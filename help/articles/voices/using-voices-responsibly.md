title: Using voices responsibly
section: Voices

Use a person's voice only if it is yours or they have said yes. Voltage asks you once per voice, keeps it to its video, checks every line and labels the export.

**Whose voice you can use.** Your own voice, or the voice of someone who has given you permission to change what they say. Owning the video, or having filmed it, is not the same as their permission. Never use the voice of a public figure, a child, or anyone who has said no.

**Use this voice?** The first time Voltage makes new lines in a speaker's voice, it asks whose voice it is: This is my voice, or I have permission from this person to change what they say. Tick the box to confirm you have the rights and consent, then press Use this voice. You answer once per voice. If the wording ever changes, Voltage asks again the next time you use the voice, so your answer always matches the words you read.

**This video only.** A voice cloned from a speaker in a video works only in that video, its variants and its language versions. The voice shows a small This video only tag. In any other video Voltage says "This voice can only be used in the video it came from." and you can pick another voice.

**Saving your own voice.** To use your voice in other videos, press Save to My voices on it. Voltage opens Prove it's your voice: read a short phrase aloud, and Voltage checks that the voice reading it is the voice in the video. If it passes, the voice moves to My voices and works everywhere. If it is sent for review or does not pass, the voice stays in its video and Voltage says why. Someone else's voice cannot be saved to My voices.

**Before you export.** When an export has a cloned voice, Voltage shows one short reminder with two boxes:

- **Add an "AI voice" label to the video (recommended).** On by default. A small AI voice tag sits in the corner for the first 3 seconds, and the export's description file says the voice was made with AI. You can switch it off here.
- **I have permission from everyone whose voice was changed.** Tick it to export.

One reminder covers every voice in the export, and a batch export asks once for the whole batch. Every export also carries permanent details saying it contains an AI-generated voice made with Voltage, with its export id and the time. Do not remove them.

**Held and blocked lines.** Voltage checks every line before it is voiced, in any voice. Lines that ask for payment details, passwords or codes, pretend to be a real person, a bank or an official, take sides in an election, are sexual, or threaten or harass someone are not voiced: you see "I can't voice this line." with the reason and Edit the line. Health and money claims, and lines Voltage is unsure about, are held for a quick check by a person at Voltage. You get a notification when it is cleared, usually the same working day. Three blocked lines in a day pause voicing with cloned voices on your account until someone at Voltage looks; contact support if that is a mistake.

**Report misuse.** If you find a voice used without consent, or anything harmful made with Voltage, use Report misuse on the shared link, or the form at the foot of the Acceptable use policy. You do not need an account. Tell us where it is and what is wrong. Someone at Voltage reads every report, can trace an export back to the account and voice behind it, and can turn off links, disable the voice and pause cloning for the account.

Where these steps are not switched on for your account yet, voices are cloned only after the speaker reads a phrase aloud, as in The speaker's own voice and consent.
