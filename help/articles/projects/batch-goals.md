On a project page, **Ask Voltage about all videos** takes one goal, like "make every offer line say 25% off", and works on every video in the project with it. The first time, it shows the three steps and a few examples under the box (**Try:**); press one to put it in the box, change it if you like, then press **Plan it**.

Pick the videos first. Every video is ticked; untick the ones to leave alone. When your account has stages, each one shows its stage (Draft, In review, Approved…).

Voltage plans once and shows one card: how many videos, how many lines the goal names, about what it costs and about how long it takes, like "5 videos · 11 lines · about $0.42 · about 2 min". The cost comes from your own earlier turns and the voice prices; the time from earlier batches. When there is nothing measured yet to go on, that part is left out rather than guessed. The card also says the most you pay for all of it: each video's own turn cap, added up. A batch that could take the project past its ceiling does not start; raise the ceiling on the project page or pick fewer videos.

Nothing runs until you press **Go** (it says how many videos it starts). Then each video gets one ordinary Voltage turn, three at a time, and the board shows one sentence on what is happening ("Done. 2 videos have changes to review and 1 video had nothing to change.") and a row per video: Queued, Working, Ready to review, Nothing to change or Failed (with the reason). A video that needs your answer shows **Waiting on you** with its question; answer it right there and that video carries on, while the others never wait for it. Select a video's name, or **Hear the changes in** under a video that is ready, to open it and hear its lines.

A batch never adds anything to a video by itself. **Add all that passed** adds every proposal whose take passed its checks, in every video, each video as its own add. Each video then has its own **Undo** for what the batch added there.

**Cancel the rest** stops the videos still queued; the ones already working finish the step they are on. Spend shows in Usage under each video and the project, and the board's total is exactly what the videos spent.

**Fix in all** in the project check works the same way: the issue becomes the goal, in the videos it names.
